The honest caveat first: you list advanced German as compulsory and mine is B1, in classes,
heading for B2. Everything below is the argument for why the energy and climate side might

I spent my Master's thesis inside exactly this policy space. Using Germany's April 2023
nuclear phase-out as a natural experiment, I assembled about 140,000 observations from
ENTSO-E, Network Transparency and futures market data and asked what the phase-out actually
did to renewable dispatch. The answer was more interesting than the expected one: no
aggregate effect, but a significant increase in onshore wind generation — most plausibly
through reduced congestion in the northwest — and higher average day-ahead prices, implying
a net welfare loss. Distinguishing that congestion mechanism from the competing explanation,
a falling market value for renewables, took interacted regression models and a spatial
analysis across all four TSO zones.

So I already work in the units of your practice: dispatch, congestion, day-ahead prices,
TSO zones, and the gap between what a policy was meant to do and what the data says it did.

Second-year unit volumes came in above the first while revenue slipped slightly; the useful
work was identifying pricing dynamics as the cause and telling that to studio CEOs instead
of the headline they were hoping for.

I am a year into my career and not pretending otherwise. What I would point to is that the
