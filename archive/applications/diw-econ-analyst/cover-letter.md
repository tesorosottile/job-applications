My Master's thesis at LMU is the closest thing I have to the work you do. I used Germany's
April 2023 nuclear phase-out as a natural experiment and built a panel of roughly 140,000
observations from ENTSO-E, Network Transparency and futures market data. The aggregate
result was a null — no measurable change in total renewable dispatch — but onshore wind
generation rose significantly and day-ahead prices went up, which points to a net welfare
loss. Two mechanisms could explain that: a reduced market value of renewables, or reduced
grid congestion. I separated them with interacted regression models and a spatial congestion
analysis across all four TSO zones, rather than choosing the tidier story. I wrote the
propensity score matching and OLS routines myself in Python.

That is a policy question answered with data, which is what your descriptions of the work
describe, and the German energy market is the system I already know best.
