This posting is written for 2027 Bachelor's graduates and I am neither — I finished an
Economics MSc at LMU Munich in 2024 and have spent a year working since. I am applying

Competition analysis turns on establishing which mechanism explains what you observe, and
that is what my thesis was built to do. Using Germany's April 2023 nuclear phase-out as a
natural experiment, I assembled roughly 140,000 observations from ENTSO-E, Network
Transparency and futures market data. Aggregate renewable dispatch did not move, but onshore
wind generation rose significantly and day-ahead prices went up. Two candidate explanations
competed — a reduced market value of renewables, or reduced grid congestion — and I
separated them with interacted regression models rather than asserting the more convenient
one. The propensity score matching and OLS routines were written from scratch in Python
rather than called from a library I could not inspect.

That is the analyst's job in miniature: a large messy dataset, two rival stories, and a
result that has to survive someone attacking the identification.

The year since has been commercial rather than academic — sole accountability for a
product's strategy at around €34,000 in average monthly net sales, with the analysis
reported to external CEOs. It taught me to defend a finding to people who wanted a
different one, which I suspect matters in front of a regulator.

I am 24 with a year of experience and no competition-law background. What I have is
independent empirical work and a year of owning something outright.
