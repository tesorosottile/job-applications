# Narrative Library

Four questions every application implicitly asks. Written once, reused. **Edit these until
they sound like you** — they are drafts in your voice, not final copy. Drafting tasks adapt
these; they don't rewrite them from scratch.

---

## N1 — Why consulting, coming from a games company?

Because rcp *is* a consulting firm, and because the part of the job I was best at was the
consulting part. My remit was one product, but the work was diagnosis: why volume was holding
while revenue slipped, what the data said versus what everyone assumed, what to tell a studio
CEO who wanted a different answer. I did that with a marketing brief attached to it. I'd
rather do it without one.

The longer version is that I came out of an economics MSc wanting to use it, took the best
role available in the city I wanted to stay in, and got something more valuable than I
expected — ownership at an age where most people are executing tasks. But the thread from my
thesis to here is analytical work on questions where the answer isn't obvious. Consulting is
where that thread goes next.

## N2 — Why leave rcp?

My contract runs to February 2027 and I've learned what this role has to teach me. I have
commercial ownership of one product in one industry; what I want next is more surface area —
more data, more sectors, more structure, and colleagues who are better than me at the thing
I'm trying to get better at. That's not available where I am, and it isn't a complaint about
where I am.

*(Tone check: no grievance, no over-explaining. Two sentences is usually enough.)*

## N3 — Why this firm?

*Slot-filled per application. The drafting task must ground this in something specific and
verifiable — a named practice, a published case or report, a sector focus, a methodology —
and must cite where it found it. If it can only produce generic praise, leave the paragraph
out entirely rather than pad it.*

Template shape: one sentence on the specific thing → one sentence on why it connects to
something I've actually done (cite evidence ID) → one sentence on what I'd want to work on.

## N4 — You have one year of experience. Why should we take you?

I know. I'm 24 and a year in, and I'm not going to pretend that's a decade. What I'd point to
instead: in that year I was the only person accountable for a product's commercial
performance, and before that I wrote an independent empirical study on a national policy shock
using 140,000 observations and had to defend which of two mechanisms explained the result.
Both of those are the job, in miniature. I've covered a lot of ground quickly, and the thing
I'm optimising for now is working somewhere that makes that ground deeper.

## Standard objections — short answers

**German is B1.** True. I'm working towards B2 and taking classes. My working languages are
English and Italian, and I've operated professionally in Munich for over a year.

**Gaming background.** rcp is a consultancy; games are its client sector. The analytical and
commercial work transfers; the domain is incidental.

**Economics MSc but a product job title.** The title was a flexible trainee programme; the
work was commercial strategy with full ownership. I'd rather be judged on the second.
