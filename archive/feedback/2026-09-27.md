## Rules this produced

Three failure modes, all visible in the posting text, none caught by the sweep:

1. **Dead links.** McKinsey's URL resolved to a careers homepage. The sweep recorded it as a
   posting. → Sweep must now confirm the link resolves to the posting itself and reject
   redirects to landing pages.
2. **Programme pages with no application route.** Aurora's entry pointed at a Graduate
   Programme overview with no link to the actual form. → Sweep must record the direct
   application URL, or drop the row.
3. **Language requirements read too late.** Four of seven required business German —
   already excluded by `profile/filters.yaml`. The sweep surfaced them as normal shortlist
   rows, so the exclusion only bit at drafting time, after a Monday triage slot was spent on
   each. → Language, cohort and prerequisite-experience checks moved into the sweep, before
   scoring.

All three are now in `prompts/01-sourcing-sweep.md` under "Link verification" and

## The finding that matters more than any of them

**The B1 German exclusion removes most of the German market, and the target list is mostly
German.** Four of seven this week, and it will be four of seven most weeks. The pipeline is
not going to reach 80 by December against a universe that is structurally excluded.

Three ways out, and they are not mutually exclusive:

- **Reweight the universe towards English-working employers**: UK and Brussels offices of the
  economic consultancies, EU institutions, international bodies, the London and Brussels
  arms of firms already on the list. RBB is the model — a general application across offices
  sidesteps the language question entirely.
- **Take the UK sponsorship flag more seriously.** It was set as a soft priority boost at
  fit ≥ 8. If the German-speaking market is closed, UK roles stop being a lottery ticket and
  start being the main line.
- **Accelerate B2.** Guidehouse is the single best domain fit seen so far — the thesis maps
  onto it almost exactly — and the only thing blocking it is the language line. That is a
  concrete, dated reason to push the certificate rather than a vague intention.

This is for the retro to propose against, but it is large enough that it probably should not
wait until 1 October.

## FB-1 — Stated requirements are not all hard constraints

> "German shouldn't be a hard constraint if not explicitly mentioned in the job posting. I am
> achieving B2 soon enough that I should be considered at least respectable in that."

> "Anything that is just 'preferable' in a job posting should be considered preferable, not a
> hard constraint. Just like the CRA thing. That might be the difference between a 9.5 and a
> 10, but nothing more than that. What matters more are my goals/ambitions."

The rule to encode: **a preference costs fractions of a point; only an explicit, unambiguous
hard requirement excludes.** And the exclusion is a *flag on a surfaced row*, not a silent
drop — he decides, the sweep does not.

Concretely, for `profile/filters.yaml`:

- `business_german_required: exclude` → **too blunt.** Replace the binary with a graded
  penalty. Suggested: German unmentioned → no penalty. "Advantageous / a plus / nice to have"
  → −0.5. "Good German" → −1. "Fluent / business level / verhandlungssicher" → −1.5 and
  `language_flag` set, but the row still surfaces.
- Same principle for cohort and prerequisite-experience statements. CRA's "2027 Bachelor's
  graduates" scored as a −3 and forced `status = no`; under the new rule it is a penalty and
  a flag, and he chooses.
- The sweep must keep **reading** these requirements at sweep time — that part of the
  2026-09-27 hardening stands. What changes is the consequence: annotate and score, do not
  exclude.

**Why this is his call and not the system's:** B2 is close enough that a "good German"
requirement is a negotiation rather than a wall, and an application costs him ten minutes of
review against a real option value. The earlier rule optimised for not wasting drafts. He is
telling me the scarcer resource is opportunities, not drafts.

**One honest caveat for the retro to weigh.** A stated *eligibility* is not always a
preference. CRA's cohort line is in the job title and defines the intake; a graduate-scheme
ATS may filter on graduation year before a human reads anything. Encoding "preferences are
preferences" is right; assuming every stated restriction is soft is not. Suggested
distinction for `filters.yaml`: a **capability** statement (language level, tools, domain
knowledge) is always scored, never excluding. An **eligibility** statement (graduating
cohort, degree class, right-to-work) is scored but carries a visible `eligibility_flag` so
the triage card shows what is being overridden.

`profile/filters.yaml` is PROPOSABLE, so I have not changed it. This is written for the
1 October retro to propose against — but it is the highest-value change on the list and
should not wait if Monday's sweep is going to use the old rule.
