## Rules this produced

Three failure modes, all visible in the posting text, none caught by the sweep:

1. **Dead links.** McKinsey's URL resolved to a careers homepage. The sweep recorded it as a
   posting. → Sweep must now confirm the link resolves to the posting itself and reject
   redirects to landing pages.
2. **Programme pages with no application route.** Aurora's entry pointed at a Graduate
   Programme overview with no link to the actual form. → Sweep must record the direct
   application URL, or drop the row.
3. **Language requirements read too late.** Four of seven required business German —
   already excluded by `profile/filters.yaml`. The sweep surfaced them as normal shortlist
   rows, so the exclusion only bit at drafting time, after a Monday triage slot was spent on
   each. → Language, cohort and prerequisite-experience checks moved into the sweep, before
   scoring.

All three are now in `prompts/01-sourcing-sweep.md` under "Link verification" and
"Requirement checks that must happen at sweep time".

## The finding that matters more than any of them

**The B1 German exclusion removes most of the German market, and the target list is mostly
German.** Four of seven this week, and it will be four of seven most weeks. The pipeline is
not going to reach 80 by December against a universe that is structurally excluded.

Three ways out, and they are not mutually exclusive:

- **Reweight the universe towards English-working employers**: UK and Brussels offices of the
  economic consultancies, EU institutions, international bodies, the London and Brussels
  arms of firms already on the list. RBB is the model — a general application across offices
  sidesteps the language question entirely.
- **Take the UK sponsorship flag more seriously.** It was set as a soft priority boost at
  fit ≥ 8. If the German-speaking market is closed, UK roles stop being a lottery ticket and
  start being the main line.
- **Accelerate B2.** Guidehouse is the single best domain fit seen so far — the thesis maps
  onto it almost exactly — and the only thing blocking it is the language line. That is a
  concrete, dated reason to push the certificate rather than a vague intention.

This is for the retro to propose against, but it is large enough that it probably should not
wait until 1 October.

## Note on the drafting rule

The standing rule is that a business-German posting stops and sets `flagged-language`. I
followed it rather than drafting the four anyway. If you would rather apply to some of them
regardless — DIW Econ and Guidehouse are both strong enough to be worth a long shot — that
is a deliberate override, not a filter change, and the letters would need a paragraph
addressing the language gap head-on.
